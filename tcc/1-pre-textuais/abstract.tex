The Brazilian semiarid region is characterized by high rainfall variability, significant evaporation losses and recurrent droughts, which make reservoir operation relevant to water security. This study aimed to develop and evaluate a web-based decision support system built on monthly water-balance simulation for analysing the operation of single reservoirs and multi-reservoir systems in Ceará, Brazil. The simulator represents individual, series and parallel operation and applies monthly rule curves with rationing; the application, built with React, FastAPI and SQLite, covers 155 reservoirs and allows users to configure analyses and export the monthly records. The implementation was verified against an independent spreadsheet calculation and through checks of the rationing rules, applied to the main reservoirs of 25 reservoir systems and to 33,108 monthly balances, through an independent recalculation of the series and parallel modes over 5,280 monthly balances, and through twelve controlled test cases. The independent calculations and the simulator agreed in all cases, with differences equal to zero within a numerical tolerance of 10\textsuperscript{-9}~hm\textsuperscript{3}, all controlled cases passed, and the error of the monthly scheme, estimated against an analytical solution and daily sub-steps, was below 0.3\%. In the sensitivity analysis, evaporation was the most influential parameter for failures, followed by demand; the transfer trigger and rate showed safety thresholds for the receiving reservoir. The applications showed that, at the Flor do Campo reservoir, where evaporation consumes 83\% of the inflow, the operating rule of the system's proactive drought management plan reduced the months with failure from 188 to 106 and the longest failure sequence from 20 to 10 months, but, with rationing in 80\% of the months, raised the undelivered volume from 22.66 to 74.03 hm\textsuperscript{3}, and 94\% of the water saved evaporated: the rule redistributes scarcity without increasing the available water; that the failures in the Carnaubal--Batalhão system occurred when the secondary reservoir lacked storage to take over the whole joint demand; and that, in the Fogareiro--Quixeramobim system, with no failures and drought-state time shares close to those of the plan, an optimistic result because the downstream inflow does not respond to upstream operation, the trigger rule caused transfers to switch on and off in consecutive months. Since no comparison with observed storage was performed, the tests verify the implementation but do not validate how well the model represents actual operation.
