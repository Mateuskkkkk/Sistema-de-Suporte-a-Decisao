The Brazilian semiarid region is characterized by high rainfall variability, significant evaporation losses and recurrent droughts, which make reservoir operation relevant to water security. This study aimed to develop and evaluate a web-based decision support system built on monthly water-balance simulation for analysing the operation of single reservoirs and multi-reservoir systems in Ceará, Brazil. The simulator represents individual, series and parallel operation and applies monthly rule curves with rationing; the application, built with React, FastAPI and SQLite, covers 155 reservoirs and allows users to configure analyses and export the monthly records. The implementation was verified against an independent spreadsheet calculation and through checks of the rationing rules, applied to the main reservoirs of 25 reservoir systems and to 33,108 monthly balances, through an independent recalculation of the series and parallel modes over 5,280 monthly balances, and through twelve controlled test cases. The independent calculations and the simulator agreed in all cases, with differences equal to zero within a numerical tolerance of 10\textsuperscript{-9}~hm\textsuperscript{3}, all controlled cases passed, and the error of the monthly scheme, estimated against an analytical solution and daily sub-steps, was below 0.3\%. In the one-at-a-time sensitivity analysis, evaporation was, at Flor do Campo and per unit of relative change, the most influential parameter for failures, followed by demand; the transfer trigger and rate showed safety thresholds for the receiving reservoir. At the Flor do Campo reservoir, where evaporation consumes 83\% of the inflow, the operating rule of the system's proactive drought management plan reduced the months with failure from 188 to 106 and the longest failure sequence from 20 to 10 months, but, with rationing in 80\% of the months, raised the volume not delivered by the reservoir from 22.66 to 74.03 hm\textsuperscript{3}, and 94\% of the water saved evaporated: at the reservoir, the rule redistributes scarcity without increasing the available water. In the Carnaubal--Batalhão system, the failures occurred when the secondary reservoir lacked storage to take over the whole joint demand. In the Fogareiro--Quixeramobim system, there were no failures, the drought-state time shares were close to those of the plan, and the trigger rule caused transfers to switch on and off in consecutive months. This result is optimistic, because the downstream inflow does not respond to upstream operation. Since no comparison with observed storage was performed, the tests verify the implementation but do not validate how well the model represents actual operation.
